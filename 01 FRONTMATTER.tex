\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries Abstract}
\end{center}
\vspace{1em}
\noindent

We apply the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) to cardinality-constrained portfolio selection, a combinatorial problem that becomes NP-hard when an investor must choose exactly $K$ assets from a universe of $N$ candidates while minimizing portfolio volatility. The problem is cast as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) and solved using a parameterized quantum circuit on Qiskit simulators. Covariance matrices are estimated from crisis-period market data—covering the 2008 Global Financial Crisis, the 2020 COVID-19 crash, and the 2022 inflation shock—to stress-test the algorithm under realistic conditions of elevated volatility and correlation breakdown. We benchmark QAOA against exact brute-force search as a function of circuit depth $p$ and discuss the approximation ratio, sensitivity to the penalty hyperparameter $\lambda$, and prospects for near-term quantum hardware deployment.
