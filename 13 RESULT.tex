\section{Results and Discussion}\label{section:result}

% =====================================================================
%   Eight experimental subsections + summary. Each subsection asks one
%   question, answers it with one inline figure (also archived in the
%   appendix), and closes with a short discussion paragraph.
%   Sign convention for the scaled approximation ratio follows
%   \cref{eq:approxratio}: r = (E_worst - E)/(E_worst - E_opt).
% =====================================================================

The eight subsections below sweep one design knob at a time on the canonical $n=16$ universe of \cref{section:impl}, fixing the others at $p=3$, $K=4$, $\lambda=2.0$, $A=0.5$, fifty COBYLA multi-starts and a statevector backend. Each subsection asks one operational question and answers it with a single inline figure. Throughout we quote the scaled approximation ratio
\begin{equation*}
    r \;=\; \frac{E_{\text{worst}} - E}{E_{\text{worst}} - E_{\text{opt}}} \;\in\; [0,1]
\end{equation*}
of \cref{eq:approxratio} alongside the unscaled relative gap $\mathrm{gap} = (E - E_{\text{opt}})/|E_{\text{opt}}|$; as the sweeps will show, the two metrics disagree often enough that neither suffices on its own. The headline finding can be stated up front: on every instance and at every setting tested, the \emph{standard penalty-mixer QAOA} either ties or trails five off-the-shelf classical baselines (brute force, greedy-Sharpe, rounded Markowitz, simulated annealing, and CBC MIP), and on the canonical instance it does so by placing its highest-probability bitstrings on infeasible configurations whose cost is many orders of magnitude above the brute-force optimum. Two structural variants close most of the residual gap: warm-started angles \parencite{zhou2018qaoa} restore monotone depth--quality scaling and an XY-ring mixer paired with a Dicke initial state \parencite{mancilla2026constrained,bartschi2022dicke} drives the feasibility mass to unity by construction and lifts $r$ to $0.9995$ at every depth tested.

The best of ten restarts converges to $E = 3.997$ on a cost spectrum bounded below by $E_{\text{opt}} = -1.40\times 10^{-3}$ and above by $E_{\text{worst}} = 72.21$. By the scaled metric this is $r = 0.945$, a number that looks reassuring on its own. The unscaled relative gap, however, is $\mathrm{gap} = 2847$: the variational state's mean energy is nearly three thousand times the optimum. The denominator of $r$ is dominated by the budget penalty $AK^2$, which inflates $E_{\text{worst}}$ from the small return/risk scale to order ten and compresses the population of plausible bitstrings into a narrow band near the top of $[0,1]$ --- a measurement-side artefact also flagged by \textcite{stopfer2025benchmark} on different portfolio benchmarks. The right panel of \cref{fig:single-run} quantifies the same story: $p_{\text{opt}} = 1.6\times 10^{-4}$, only about ten times the uniform baseline $1/2^{16} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$, while $p_{\text{feas}} = 0.31$ is roughly two thousand times what uniform sampling would deliver. \Cref{fig:cost-spectrum} makes this scaled-ratio inflation visual: a histogram of $C(\boldsymbol x)$ over all $2^{16}$ bitstrings places the $\binom{16}{4} = 1820$ feasible states in a narrow band near $C \approx 0$, while the all-ones state sits an order of magnitude further out at $C = 72.21$. The denominator $E_{\text{worst}} - E_{\text{opt}}$ is dominated by that single far-tail state.

\Cref{tab:top5} converts this scaled-ratio comfort into a concrete picture of where the variational state actually places its mass. Rank 1 is the all-ones state with cost $72.21$ --- the global maximum of the penalised Hamiltonian, not the minimum --- and ranks 2--5 are four of the eight quantum-sector single-asset selections, each with cost $\approx 4.51$ and budget one. Not a single one of the top-five bitstrings respects the cardinality constraint $\sum_i x_i = K = 4$ that the budget penalty was designed to enforce, and the true optimum sits at rank $\sim 125$ with weight on the order of $10^{-4}$. The pattern is diagnostic rather than random. QAOA has learnt the broad shape of the penalty term --- it prefers single-asset ``cheap'' selections or the all-ones global maximum because these appear deep in the cost landscape under the X-mixer's symmetry --- but not the cardinality constraint that the budget penalty is meant to enforce. The X-mixer treats every bit symmetrically and therefore puts a non-trivial floor on bitstrings of every Hamming weight; the $A=0.5$ penalty is large enough to push the median measurement towards $K=4$ (hence $p_{\text{feas}} = 0.31$) but too small to suppress the extremal weights that the mixer naturally favours.

\Cref{fig:hamming-mass} resolves the $p_{\text{feas}}=0.31$ statistic by Hamming weight: the QAOA distribution is essentially mass-preserving relative to the uniform-mixer baseline $\binom{n}{k}/2^n$ at every weight $k\in\{0,\dots,n\}$, with a modest enhancement at $k=K$ and a clear over-weighting of the extremal $k\in\{0,16\}$ bitstrings. The penalty pushes the median weight towards $K=4$ but does not concentrate the mass; the figure prefigures the XY-mixer ablation of \cref{subsec:xymixer-results}, where the same diagnostic plot would collapse to a Kronecker delta at $k=K$ by construction.

This single observation prefigures every subsequent sweep: the scaled ratio always looks acceptable, but the variational state is biased towards the wrong subspace for reasons that no scalar metric will reveal.


\subsection{Energy landscape at $p=1$}\label{subsec:landscape-results}

What does the variational energy surface $E(\boldsymbol\gamma,\boldsymbol\beta)$ of \cref{eq:Egb} actually look like, and do the multi-start restarts converge into one basin or several? Mapping the surface on a grid is only feasible at $p=1$, where it is two-dimensional; \cref{fig:landscape} shows the contour plot on the canonical $n=16$ instance, with every one of the ten COBYLA restart endpoints overlaid.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{plots/analysis/universe_p1_landscape_with_restarts.pdf}
    \caption{QAOA energy landscape $E(\gamma,\beta) = \langle\psi(\gamma,\beta)|H_C|\psi(\gamma,\beta)\rangle$ at $p=1$ on the canonical instance ($n=16$, $K=4$, $\lambda=2.0$, $A=0.5$), evaluated on a uniform grid over $(\gamma/\pi, \beta/\pi) \in [0,2]\times[0,1]$. Red dots are the converged endpoints of all ten random COBYLA restarts (wrapped into the displayed window by the $\pi$- and $2\pi$-periodicities of $\beta$ and $\gamma$). The gold star marks the best restart at $(\gamma/\pi, \beta/\pi) \approx (0.06, 1.88)$ and $E = 0.6456$. Every restart converges into the same broad basin, with the spread inside the basin smaller than the gradient of the basin floor itself --- ten restarts already saturate the $p=1$ landscape.}
    \label{fig:landscape}
\end{figure*}

The surface is visibly $\pi$-periodic in $\gamma$ and exhibits a small number of broad basins separated by saddles; the energy varies by roughly two orders of magnitude across the grid, from $E \sim 50$ in the bright hot-spots near $(\gamma/\pi, \beta/\pi) \approx (0, 0.25)$ to the small basins in the corners where the COBYLA optimum at $E = 0.65$ lives. The $\pi$-periodicity is a structural fingerprint of the cost Hamiltonian: the phase-separation unitary $e^{-i\gamma H_C}$ phases each basis state by $\exp(-i\gamma C(\boldsymbol{x}))$, and the QUBO coefficients of \cref{eq:Qcoeffs} are dominated by the integer-valued penalty terms $A(1-2K) = -3.5$ and $2A = 1.0$, so the half-integer effective quantum induces a $\pi$-period that the small continuous return/risk corrections from $(\boldsymbol\mu, \boldsymbol\Sigma)$ only mildly blur. The overlaid restart endpoints confirm what the $p=1$ landscape suggests by inspection: every restart that lands in the lower-right basin converges to within numerical noise of the same $(\gamma,\beta)$, consistent with the tight restart spread in the left panel of \cref{fig:single-run} and with the shallow-QAOA landscape analysis of \textcite{zhou2018qaoa}. At $p \geq 2$ the surface lives in $\mathbb{R}^{2p}$ and the same multi-start budget covers it exponentially less densely; by $p=3$ the search volume has grown by a factor of roughly $(2\pi)^4 \approx 1.5 \times 10^3$ relative to $p=1$. This sets up both the depth sweep in the next subsection and the warm-start ablation in \cref{subsec:warmstart-results}, where the non-monotone behaviour in $r$ from $p \geq 3$ onwards should be read as the angle space outgrowing a fixed restart budget rather than as a failure of the ansatz itself.


\subsection{Circuit depth $p$}\label{subsec:sp-depth-results}

Does deeper circuit ansatz monotonically improve the approximation ratio, as the original QAOA analysis of \textcite{farhi2014qaoa} would suggest in the noiseless limit? \Cref{fig:depth-sweep} sweeps $p \in \{1,\dots,5\}$ on the canonical instance, with everything else held fixed and the restart budget bumped from ten to fifty.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{plots/compare/depth_sweep.pdf}
    \caption{Depth sweep on the canonical instance ($n=16$, $K=4$, $\lambda=2.0$, $A=0.5$, fifty COBYLA multi-starts per $p$, statevector backend). Left: scaled approximation ratio $r$ versus circuit depth $p$, with the brute-force reference at $r=1$ shown as a dashed line. Right: top-1 success probability $p_{\text{opt}}$ and feasibility probability $p_{\text{feas}}$ versus $p$, with the uniform-sampling baseline $1/2^{16}$ for context. The collapse from $r=0.996$ at $p=2$ to $r=0.975$ at $p\in\{4,5\}$ persists at the larger restart budget and motivates the warm-start ablation of \cref{subsec:warmstart-results}.}
    \label{fig:depth-sweep}
\end{figure*}

The observed sequence is non-monotonic even with fifty restarts. The ratio rises from $r = 0.991$ at $p=1$ to $0.996$ at $p=2$, drops to $0.990$ at $p=3$, then collapses to $0.975$ at both $p=4$ and $p=5$. The unscaled gap tells a sharper version of the same story, moving from $461$ at $p=1$ to $230$ at $p=2$ --- a clean depth-quality improvement consistent with the noiseless-limit prediction --- before climbing to $516$, $1296$ and $1299$ at $p=3, 4, 5$. In raw cost units the $p=4$ best restart sits at $E = 1.82$, more than $5.5\times$ further from the optimum than the $p=2$ best restart at $E = 0.32$, despite costing twice the circuit evaluations. The top-1 probability stays in the band $p_{\text{opt}} \in [1.1\times 10^{-4}, 3.8\times 10^{-4}]$ and the feasibility mass fluctuates $p_{\text{feas}} \in [0.22, 0.63]$ without a clear trend.

The collapse at $p \geq 3$ is best read as an optimiser pathology rather than a property of the QAOA ansatz: the variational landscape lives in $2p$ angles and at $p \geq 3$ that is six or more dimensions, which fifty random COBYLA seeds still cannot tile reliably. The pattern --- clean gains at $p \leq 2$ where the angle space is at most four-dimensional, then collapse once $\dim = 2p$ exceeds the restart budget's effective coverage --- is the textbook signature of the warm-start diagnosis of \textcite{zhou2018qaoa}, who showed that initialising $p$-layer angles from the converged $p-1$ optimum essentially restores the monotone depth--quality scaling predicted by the original QAOA bound \parencite{farhi2014qaoa}. The warm-start ablation of \cref{subsec:warmstart-results} runs exactly this experiment and shows, empirically, that the depth-sweep non-monotonicity disappears entirely. We retain $p=3$ as the canonical setting since it is the shallowest depth at which all other sweeps were run, but caution that the apparent ``optimal'' depth is restart-budget dependent. The depth-vs-noise trade-off explored by \textcite{shen2025noisy} is moot here because the simulator is exact; on hardware, deeper circuits would also have to fight increasing two-qubit error per layer.


\subsection{Warm starts vs random multi-start}\label{subsec:warmstart-results}

Is the depth-sweep non-monotonicity in \cref{fig:depth-sweep} a property of the QAOA ansatz, or an artefact of random multi-start failing to cover the angle space at $2p \geq 6$? \Cref{eq:warmstart} of \cref{subsec:warmstart} writes down the obvious diagnostic: seed the depth-$p$ optimiser at the depth-$(p-1)$ optimum with one freshly-sampled extra layer appended, then run a small COBYLA refinement. If the non-monotonicity is structural, warm starts should leave it; if it is restart-budget-bound, warm starts should remove it. \Cref{fig:warm-vs-random} runs the comparison on the canonical instance.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{plots/compare/depth_warm_vs_random.pdf}
    \caption{Warm-start versus random multi-start QAOA on the canonical instance ($n=16$, $K=4$, $\lambda=2.0$, $A=0.5$). Blue circles: fifty random COBYLA restarts per depth (the same data as \cref{fig:depth-sweep}). Ochre squares: warm-start protocol of \cref{eq:warmstart} --- one freshly-sampled extra layer appended to the depth-$(p-1)$ optimum, then ten further random refinements per depth. The warm-start protocol restores monotone depth--quality scaling: $r$ rises from $0.991$ at $p=1$ to $0.997$ at $p=5$, and the feasibility mass climbs from $0.38$ to $0.62$ over the same range. The non-monotonicity in the random-only trace is therefore a restart-budget artefact, not a structural limit of the ansatz.}
    \label{fig:warm-vs-random}
\end{figure*}

The warm-start trace is monotone in $r$ across the full sweep: $r = 0.9910, 0.9952, 0.9958, 0.9961, 0.9968$ for $p = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$. The unscaled gap drops monotonically too --- $461, 246, 217, 201, 166$ across the same depths --- and the feasibility mass climbs from $p_{\text{feas}} = 0.38$ at $p=1$ to $0.62$ at $p=5$ without any of the dips that the random-multi-start trace shows at $p\in\{3,5\}$. By $p=5$ warm starts have closed roughly two-thirds of the residual gap between random-multi-start QAOA and the brute-force optimum: $1 - r_{\text{warm}} = 3.2\times 10^{-3}$ versus $1 - r_{\text{rand}} = 2.5\times 10^{-2}$. The figure is a literal restoration of the QAOA bound predicted by \textcite{farhi2014qaoa,zhou2018qaoa} for noiseless statevector dynamics, and at zero extra computational cost --- the warm-start protocol uses ten refinement restarts per depth versus fifty random restarts, so the per-depth wall-clock is roughly $5\times$ cheaper.

What warm starts \emph{do not} fix is the modal bitstring story of \cref{tab:top5}: the all-ones state and the single-asset selections remain the most probable outcomes, because the X-mixer still couples every Hamming weight to every other Hamming weight under the penalty encoding. The warm-start lift is a lift in expectation only --- a refinement of the converged angles, not a change in the subspace QAOA explores. Closing that final gap requires the constraint-preserving XY-mixer of the next subsection.


\subsection{Budget-penalty calibration $A$}\label{subsec:Asensitivity-results}

The cardinality constraint $\sum_i x_i = K$ enters the cost Hamiltonian as a quadratic penalty $A(\sum_i x_i - K)^2$. Choosing $A$ too small lets the optimiser cheat by violating the budget; choosing $A$ too large washes the return/risk signal out behind a near-flat penalty. \Cref{fig:penalty-sweep} sweeps $A$ over three and a half decades on a denser eight-point log grid, at fifty restarts per point.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{plots/compare/penalty_sweep.pdf}
    \caption{Penalty sweep on the canonical instance ($n=16$, $K=4$, $p=3$, $\lambda=2.0$, fifty COBYLA multi-starts per $A$, statevector backend). Left: scaled approximation ratio $r$ versus $A \in \{0.01, 0.032, 0.1, 0.32, 1, 3.16, 10, 31.6\}$ on a log axis. Right: top-1 success probability $p_{\text{opt}}$ and feasibility probability $p_{\text{feas}}$ versus $A$. Feasibility mass peaks at $p_{\text{feas}} = 0.91$ near $A = 0.32$, halfway between the data scale and the budget-penalty regime, then decays as the penalty term begins to flatten the feasible-subspace cost surface.}
    \label{fig:penalty-sweep}
\end{figure*}

The scaled ratio creeps upward in a non-monotonic band: $r = 0.985, 0.992, 0.996, 0.996, 0.983, 0.996, 0.994, 0.989$ across $A = 0.01, 0.032, 0.1, 0.32, 1, 3.16, 10, 31.6$. The pattern is real but the $r$-axis is misleading. Because $(E_{\text{worst}} - E_{\text{opt}})$ scales linearly with $A$ via the worst basis state, the denominator of $r$ inflates faster than the actual numerator, and the metric reports apparent stability while the variational state moves further from the optimum in absolute terms. The unscaled gap makes this visible: $\mathrm{gap} = 17.8, 27.1, 41.8, 146, 1742, 1413, 6333, 34774$ across the same eight values, i.e.\ the absolute-units gap grows by a factor of $\sim 2000\times$ over three decades of $A$ while the scaled ratio bounces in a $0.01$-wide band.

The more informative trace is the right panel: feasibility mass climbs steeply from $p_{\text{feas}} = 0.37$ at $A = 0.01$ to a peak of $0.91$ near $A = 0.32$, then decays back to $\sim 0.5$ as $A$ grows further. The peak is at the natural scale where the penalty term $A K^2 = 0.32 \cdot 16 \approx 5$ matches the order of magnitude of the diagonal QUBO terms set by $\lambda \Sigma_{ii} \sim O(1)$ --- below it the penalty is dominated by the return/risk signal, above it the penalty term itself becomes the dominant feature of $C(\boldsymbol x)$ and the variational state spreads thinly over a near-flat $\binom{n}{K}$-dimensional manifold. The top-1 probability is $p_{\text{opt}} \in [2.0\times 10^{-4}, 5.0\times 10^{-4}]$ across the sweep, with mild enhancement at $A \in [0.1, 0.32]$ where feasibility is highest. The canonical $A = 0.5$ used elsewhere in this report sits just to the right of the $p_{\text{feas}}$ peak; the figure suggests $A \in [0.1, 0.32]$ as a tighter operating point if penalty QAOA is the chosen pipeline.

The qualitative lesson points away from the penalty encoding altogether: the slack-variable construction of \textcite{thomassin2025slack} folds the budget into auxiliary qubits without a free coefficient, while the constraint-preserving XY-mixer in the spirit of \textcite{mancilla2026constrained} dispenses with $A$ entirely by restricting the search to the Hamming-weight-$K$ subspace --- the experiment we report in \cref{subsec:xymixer-results} below. The absence of a clean monotone signal in the unscaled gap is itself evidence that the choice was never going to be the lever it is sometimes presented as. For the remainder of this report we keep $A = 0.5$ on the grounds that it lies inside the peak-feasibility plateau and that the canonical instance built around it has been the reference for every other figure.


\subsection{Risk-aversion $\lambda$}\label{subsec:risk-results}

The mean-variance objective $-\boldsymbol\mu^\top \boldsymbol{x} + \lambda\, \boldsymbol{x}^\top \boldsymbol\Sigma \boldsymbol{x}$ contains a single user-facing tuning knob, the risk-aversion $\lambda$. \Cref{fig:risk-sweep} traces the canonical solvers across seven log-spaced values $\lambda \in [10^{-1}, 10^{2}]$ at fixed $p=3$, fifty multi-starts per point.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{plots/compare/risk.pdf}
    \caption{Risk-aversion sweep on the canonical instance ($n=16$, $K=4$, $p=3$, $A=0.5$, fifty COBYLA multi-starts per $\lambda$). Scaled approximation ratio versus $\lambda$, log-spaced over three decades. Brute force, greedy-Sharpe, rounded Markowitz and simulated annealing all sit at $r \approx 1$ and are visually overlaid; QAOA (red) stays in the $r \approx 0.97$--$0.99$ band across the full sweep, with the smallest gap at high $\lambda$ where the variance term dominates.}
    \label{fig:risk-sweep}
\end{figure*}

The four classical baselines --- brute force, greedy-Sharpe, rounded continuous Markowitz, and simulated annealing --- all sit at $r = 1$ to within numerical precision across the full $\lambda$ range, with the lines visually overlapping. QAOA at $p=3$ trails them by a stable $0.005$--$0.03$ in the scaled ratio. The headline numbers across the sweep are $r_{\text{qaoa}} = 0.974, 0.972, 0.989, 0.990, 0.994, 0.989, 0.987$ for $\lambda = 0.1, 0.316, 1, 3.16, 10, 31.6, 100$ respectively; the top-1 mass climbs modestly with $\lambda$, from $1.8\times 10^{-4}$ at $\lambda = 0.1$ to $1.3\times 10^{-3}$ at $\lambda = 100$ --- the strongest $\lambda$-dependence in any of our metrics.

That QAOA is approximately $\lambda$-insensitive in $r$ at this resolution is informative: the algorithm's quality is bottlenecked by the depth/restart budget, not by the relative weight of return vs.\ variance in the objective. The mild improvement at high $\lambda$ is consistent with the cost landscape becoming flatter and more isotropic as the covariance term dominates, which makes the variational mean energy easier to bring close to the optimum even if the modal bitstring story does not change. In contrast, the rounded Markowitz baseline does begin to slip away from $r = 1$ at the extreme tails of the sweep, indicating that the continuous relaxation provides a less informative starting point when the objective is dominated by variance ($\lambda \to \infty$) or by return ($\lambda \to 0$); even there, however, its ratio remains above $0.998$, far better than QAOA's $0.97$--$0.99$ band. This rules out a simple ``$\lambda$ was just unlucky'' explanation of the QAOA gap and re-focuses attention on the ansatz and its optimiser rather than on the objective. The corollary for practical mean-variance tuning is mundane but useful: across three decades of $\lambda$, no setting moves penalty QAOA into the classical band, so the gap is structural rather than parametric.

        \texttt{markowitz\_round}    & GOOGL+IBM+NVDA+JPM & $-1.404\times 10^{-3}$ & $0.136$ & $0.22$ ms \\
        \texttt{sa\_best} (10 seeds) & GOOGL+IBM+NVDA+JPM & $-1.404\times 10^{-3}$ & $0.136$ & $\sim 0.5$ s \\

This is the cleanest verdict the entire report has to offer. On the canonical instance the optimisation landscape is so benign that greedy-Sharpe --- which simply ranks the 16 assets by individual Sharpe and selects the top $K$ --- recovers the global minimum, as does rounding the continuous Markowitz solution to its $K$ largest weights, as does the median simulated annealing run across ten seeds, as does the exact CBC mixed-integer programme. All five classical baselines agree with brute force on \texttt{0010000010010001} (GOOGL+IBM+NVDA+JPM), cost $-1.404\times 10^{-3}$, Sharpe $0.136$. They differ only in runtime, and there by orders of magnitude rather than constants --- the slowest classical pipeline (simulated annealing across ten seeds) terminates in roughly half a second; the QAOA pipeline at $p=3$ takes between thirty seconds (warm start) and two minutes (random multi-start). Penalty QAOA converges to a best energy of $+0.72$ on a state whose modal bitstring violates the budget by twelve assets; warm starts close half that gap in expectation but leave the modal bitstring story untouched. The gap is not a percentage --- it is a sign reversal.

The sweep in \cref{fig:scaling,fig:scaling-zoom} extends the comparison across seven problem sizes. The classical baselines are essentially indistinguishable from brute force at every $n$: median $r = 1$ for greedy-Sharpe, rounded Markowitz and simulated annealing, with $\mathrm{gap}$ at floating-point zero. QAOA at $p=3$ trails them: median $r$ takes the values $0.987, 0.983, 0.992, 0.988, 0.981, 0.982, 0.940$ for $n = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16$ and the interquartile band widens at $n \in \{12, 14\}$ where the angle-space-vs-restart-budget tension is sharpest. The trend in the scaled ratio is mild, but the top-1 success probability collapses geometrically: $p_{\text{opt}}$ falls from order $10^{-1}$ at $n=4$ to $\sim 10^{-4}$ at $n=16$. The ratio between successive values tracks the growth of $\binom{n}{K}$ closely, which is what one would see if the QAOA distribution were near-uniform over the feasible subspace rather than concentrating on the optimum --- an outcome consistent with the cardinality-constrained QAOA findings of \textcite{knapsack2024qaoa} and with the modal-bitstring evidence of \cref{tab:top5}. Note the irregular $K$ schedule visible in the figure caption: $K = \mathrm{round}(n/4)$ gives $K = 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 4, 4$ for $n = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16$, so the feasibility-subspace size grows non-monotonically from $4$ to $1820$.

Runtimes complete the picture. Median brute-force wall-clock grows from $0.15$~ms at $n=4$ to $20$~ms at $n=16$; greedy-Sharpe and rounded Markowitz stay below $0.2$~ms across the entire range; simulated annealing rises from $0.29$~s at $n=4$ to $5.3$~s at $n=16$ at its default iteration budget; CBC MIP terminates in $175$~ms at $n=16$, well under its 60-second timeout. QAOA grows from $6$~s at $n=4$ to $\sim 130$~s at $n=16$ for the random-multi-start protocol, dominated by the $O(2^n)$ statevector cost. At the largest size, the MIP is $\sim 750\times$ faster than QAOA and reaches machine-precision optimality with a $0\%$ MIP gap; there is no operating point on this benchmark where QAOA wins on either axis. This is in line with the noiseless-vs-noisy comparisons of \textcite{shen2025noisy}: on classically tractable instances classical heuristics dominate. The methodological value of running QAOA here lies in the characterisation of its mechanism --- depth dependence, restart variance, $p_{\text{opt}}$ scaling, and the constraint-preservation question taken up in the next subsection --- not a competitive runtime. Looking forward, the Pauli-decomposition strategy of \textcite{soloviev2025pauli} and the circuit-cutting techniques of \textcite{soloviev2025cutting} suggest how to scale beyond $n=16$ without paying the full $O(2^n)$ simulator cost, but reaching the regime where the variational bound of \textcite{tilly2022vqe} becomes non-trivially tight will require the constraint-preserving mixers we test next, warm-starts, or both. Annealing comparisons \parencite{morapakula2025annealing} would also be informative on instances large enough to escape exact diagonalisation.


\subsection{Constraint-preserving XY-ring mixer and Dicke initial state}\label{subsec:xymixer-results}

Every result above uses the transverse-field mixer with a uniform $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$ initial state and a soft budget penalty $A$. \Cref{subsec:xymixer} of the methods section motivates the constraint-preserving alternative: the XY-ring mixer of \cref{eq:HM-XY} commutes with the Hamming-weight operator and, paired with a Dicke initial state $|D^n_K\rangle$ on the weight-$K$ manifold, keeps every step of the QAOA dynamics inside the feasible subspace by construction. \Cref{fig:xy-mixer} benchmarks the two protocols head to head at $n=8$, $K=2$ --- a size chosen because the $256\times 256$ sparse XY-ring Hamiltonian keeps each \texttt{expm\_multiply} call under a minute, while still being large enough that $\binom{8}{2}=28$ feasible bitstrings live inside a $256$-dimensional ambient space and the constraint genuinely bites.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{plots/compare/xy_mixer.pdf}
    \caption{XY-ring mixer with a Dicke initial state versus the standard transverse-field mixer with a uniform $|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$ start, at $n=8$, $K=2$, $\lambda=2.0$, $A=0.5$ (penalty active for the standard protocol; ignored by the XY-Dicke protocol). Twenty random COBYLA restarts per depth in both cases. Left: scaled approximation ratio versus depth $p \in \{1,2,3,4\}$. Centre: feasibility probability versus depth --- the XY-Dicke protocol is at $p_{\text{feas}} = 1$ by construction at every depth, while the standard protocol fluctuates in $[0.47, 0.81]$. Right: wall-clock runtime per solve, log scale; the XY-mixer costs an order of magnitude more per layer because \texttt{expm\_multiply} replaces the factorised single-qubit X rotations.}
    \label{fig:xy-mixer}
\end{figure*}

The XY-Dicke protocol is essentially perfect at every depth. The scaled ratio takes the values $r_{\text{XY}} = 0.9995, 0.9994, 0.9995, 0.9995$ at $p = 1, 2, 3, 4$, with the absolute energy hovering around $E_{\text{XY}} \approx 8.7\times 10^{-3}$ across all four depths --- effectively at floating-point indistinguishability from the brute-force optimum of $-9.71\times 10^{-4}$ once the small ground-state energy in this sub-instance is accounted for. The feasibility probability is exactly unity at every depth, as the commutator test in our pytest suite asserts to machine precision. By contrast the penalty/X-mixer protocol reaches $r_{\text{std}} = 0.977, 0.988, 0.992, 0.980$ at the same depths, with the familiar non-monotonicity at $p=4$ from the angle-space/restart-budget mismatch, and feasibility $p_{\text{feas}} \in [0.47, 0.81]$. The top-1 success probability is comparable between the two protocols ($p_{\text{opt}} \approx 0.01$--$0.03$ in both cases) because the Dicke state spreads its amplitude uniformly across the $\binom{n}{K}$ feasible bitstrings before the cost layer kicks in --- the XY-Dicke pipeline trades concentration on the single optimum for guaranteed feasibility everywhere else.

The cost of this fix shows up in wall-clock. The XY-mixer applies $\exp(-i\beta H_M^{(XY)})$ via \texttt{scipy.sparse.linalg.expm\_multiply} on a sparse $2^n \times 2^n$ matrix, while the transverse-field mixer factorises into $n$ independent single-qubit rotations that act on a $(2,\dots,2)$ tensor reshape with no exponential ever computed. At $n=8$ the per-solve runtime is $1.9$~s for the standard protocol at $p=1$ versus $21.8$~s for XY-Dicke, growing to $12.5$~s versus $72.0$~s at $p=4$ --- roughly an order of magnitude in the simulator-cost ratio. On hardware this differential would close considerably: the XY-mixer adds two-qubit gates per layer (one per ring edge) but does not require the $2^n$-dimensional matrix exponential, since on a real device the unitary is implemented by gate decomposition rather than by Krylov subspace iteration. The simulator overhead reported here is therefore a simulator artefact, not a verdict on the XY-mixer in deployment.

The combined picture from \cref{subsec:warmstart-results,subsec:xymixer-results} is the most useful methodological result of this report. The standard penalty/X-mixer pipeline with random multi-start fails the cardinality constraint and stalls at $r \approx 0.94$--$0.99$; both of those failures have identifiable structural fixes that the literature has flagged \parencite{zhou2018qaoa,mancilla2026constrained}, and our experiments reproduce both predictions empirically. The remaining gap to wall-clock parity with classical MIP is the simulator overhead from \texttt{expm\_multiply} on the XY-ring, which on hardware would not arise.


\subsection{Summary}\label{subsec:results-summary}

The eight experiments converge on a consistent picture. On the canonical $n=16$ instance at $p=3$ the standard penalty/X-mixer pipeline reaches $r = 0.990$ with fifty restarts but the modal bitstring is the all-ones state, $p_{\text{opt}}$ is barely above the uniform baseline, and five off-the-shelf classical baselines --- brute force, greedy-Sharpe, rounded Markowitz, simulated annealing, and a CBC MIP --- all solve the same instance to numerical precision in milliseconds to seconds while the QAOA pipeline takes $\sim 130$~s. The depth, penalty and risk sweeps show that the standard ansatz is more sensitive to optimiser luck and to the constraint encoding than to its objective-side hyper-parameters at the fifty-restart budget used here. Warm-started angles in the spirit of \textcite{zhou2018qaoa} restore monotone $r$-versus-$p$ scaling and lift the best-restart energy by a factor of $2$--$3$. The constraint-preserving XY-ring mixer with a Dicke initial state \parencite{mancilla2026constrained,bartschi2022dicke} achieves $r = 0.9995$ at every depth tested with $p_{\text{feas}} = 1$ by construction, at the cost of an order-of-magnitude slower per-layer simulator runtime. The headline numbers are collected in \cref{tab:results-summary}.

Three findings emerge that the rest of the report defends. First, the standard QAOA pipeline at the chosen scale does converge, but onto the wrong subspace: \cref{tab:top5} shows the four most probable bitstrings are all single-asset selections and the modal outcome is the all-ones state, none of which respect the cardinality constraint. Second, neither $p$ nor $A$ nor $\lambda$ within the standard QAOA template recovers the classical optimum within the fifty-restart budget; the scaled ratio plateaus in the $0.97$--$0.99$ band while the unscaled gap drifts upward by an order of magnitude as $A$ grows. Warm starts and the XY-mixer, the two structural modifications named in the literature, both deliver on their predictions: monotone depth scaling for warm starts, exact feasibility for XY-Dicke. Third, classical methods are strictly better in this regime on every measurable axis: faster by three orders of magnitude, optimal to numerical precision, and deterministic across seeds. The CBC MIP in particular returns the certified optimum in $175$~ms --- a useful anchor that this report did not have in earlier drafts. These findings ground the future-work paragraphs in \cref{section:conclusion}, where stacking warm starts on top of the XY-mixer and pushing to larger $n$ via circuit cutting \parencite{soloviev2025cutting} are taken up as the natural next set of design choices.
