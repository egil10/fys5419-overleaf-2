\section{Implementation and Datasets}\label{section:impl}

\subsection{Code structure}\label{subsec:codestructure}

The implementation is a small collection of pure-NumPy / SciPy modules, written from scratch rather than on top of a high-level VQA library so that every conventional choice (sign of the cost, qubit ordering, scaling of the approximation ratio) is explicit and auditable. The package layout mirrors the pipeline of \cref{section:methods}.

\paragraph{Modules.} The \emph{data layer} (\texttt{data.py}) loads cached daily-close Parquet files and assembles $(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})$. The \emph{problem layer} (\texttt{portfolio.py}, \texttt{ising.py}) fixes the canonical cost $C(\boldsymbol{x})$ via \texttt{PortfolioProblem} and \texttt{DEFAULTS}, and performs the $x_i = (1-z_i)/2$ substitution to build the diagonal $H_C$ and the sparse mixer $H_M$. The \emph{solver layer} bundles QAOA variants (\texttt{qaoa.py}'s \texttt{solve} and \texttt{solve\_warm} for the X-mixer, \texttt{xy\_mixer.py}/\texttt{dicke.py} for the XY-ring/Dicke variant \texttt{solve\_xy}, \texttt{optimize.py} around COBYLA) and classical baselines (\texttt{classical.py}, \texttt{mip.py}: brute force, greedy-Sharpe, Markowitz-then-round, simulated annealing, CBC MIP) behind a uniform \texttt{SolverResult} type. The \emph{orchestration layer} (\texttt{metrics.py}, \texttt{compare.py}, \texttt{plotting.py}) computes $r$, $p_{\text{opt}}$ and $p_{\text{feas}}$, and drives the figures of \cref{section:result}.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Dependencies are pinned against Python 3.11 (numpy, pandas, scipy, matplotlib, yfinance, pyarrow, pulp, pytest); the project runs unchanged on a local 3.11.x environment and on Colab. Every long-running sweep writes a JSON cache after each subset, so a disconnected kernel costs at most one record. A separate notebook produces the canonical single run, one notebook per parameter sweep drives the remaining experiments, and a final notebook emits the figures of \cref{section:result} from the cached JSONs.

The full implementation, including the core mixer kernel and the COBYLA driver, is available on \texttt{GitHub} \parencite{furnes2026fys5419}. The X-mixer factorises as $\exp(-i\beta\sum_i X_i) = \prod_i \exp(-i\beta X_i)$ so each layer is $n$ independent single-qubit rotations on a $(2,\dots,2)$ tensor reshape, $\mathcal{O}(n\cdot 2^n)$ per layer.

\paragraph{Hybrid loop.} The simulator returns $E(\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \langle\psi(\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta})|H_C|\psi(\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle$, and SciPy's COBYLA (\texttt{maxiter}\,$=200$, \texttt{rhobeg}\,$=0.1$) updates the $2p$ angles \parencite{zhou2018qaoa}. The canonical \texttt{solve} runs fifty random restarts for the headline sweeps (\cref{subsec:sp-depth-results,subsec:Asensitivity-results,subsec:risk-results}) parallelised over a \texttt{ThreadPoolExecutor}, with ten restarts per subset in the size-scaling sweep to keep the $7\times 99$ grid tractable. The warm-start variant \texttt{solve\_warm} implements \cref{eq:warmstart}: at depth $p$ it seeds COBYLA at the depth-$(p-1)$ optimum with one fresh layer appended, then refines with ten further restarts. The XY-mixer variant \texttt{solve\_xy} prepares $|D^n_K\rangle$ via \texttt{dicke.dicke\_state}, builds the sparse XY-ring with \texttt{xy\_mixer.build\_HM\_xy}, and applies each layer as $\exp(-i\beta H_M^{(XY)})|\psi\rangle$ via \texttt{scipy.sparse.linalg.expm\_multiply}, avoiding the dense exponential. Statevector simulation removes shot noise, so any non-monotonicity in $p$ in \cref{section:result} is attributable to the optimiser.

\subsection{Datasets}\label{subsec:datasets}

All price data is daily adjusted close from \texttt{yfinance}, cached locally as Parquet. Four thematic baskets feed the experiments, summarised in \cref{tab:datasets}. \subsection{Hyperparameters and conventions}\label{subsec:hyperparams}

Unless a figure caption says otherwise, every experiment uses the canonical instance: $n=16$ assets (the concatenation of all four baskets), target cardinality $K=4$, risk-aversion $\lambda=2.0$, budget penalty $A=0.5$, and circuit depth $p=3$. These are the project-wide defaults so that every notebook agrees by construction.

The classical optimiser is COBYLA with \texttt{maxiter}\,$=200$ and \texttt{rhobeg}\,$=0.1$, run from fifty random multi-starts in the depth, risk and penalty sweeps and ten random multi-starts per subset in the size-scaling sweep; SPSA is available as an alternative for noisy objectives but is not used in the reported figures. Initial parameters are drawn as $\gamma_k,\beta_k \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 2\pi]$ for all $k=1,\dots,p$. Because $\exp(-i\beta\sum_i X_i)$ is $2\pi$-periodic in $\beta$ up to a global phase, using a single common sampling range is equivalent to the more conventional $\beta\in[0,\pi]$ choice and keeps the multi-start sampler uniform. Restart budgets were deliberately bumped from the ten-restart values used in earlier drafts of this report to fifty, after a first pass showed that several apparent dips in $r$ vs $\lambda$ and $r$ vs $A$ were within restart variance; bumping to fifty removed them. The size-scaling sweep retains ten restarts per subset because the cost per QAOA call grows as $2^n p$ and the $99$-subset grid would not finish in the available budget otherwise.

Seeding follows a single master seed of $42$; restart $r$ for SPSA uses seed $42+r$, while COBYLA is deterministic given its initial point and therefore needs no additional seed. For the assets-vs-runtime scaling sweep in \cref{fig:scaling} we vary the number of random subsets per problem size as $M(n) = 20$ for $n\leq 10$, $M(12)=8$, $M(14)=10$ and $M(16)=1$, balancing statistical resolution at small $n$ against the cost of the $2^n$ brute-force reference at large $n$. The XY-mixer benchmark of \cref{subsec:xymixer-results} runs at $n=8$, $K=2$ with twenty random restarts per depth, chosen as the largest instance where \texttt{expm\_multiply} on the $256\times 256$ XY ring stays under one minute per layer.

\subsection{Validation}\label{subsec:validation}

The pipeline is guarded by a small test suite, organised so that a regression points directly at the offending layer.

\paragraph{QUBO$\to$Ising round-trip.} One check enumerates every basis state $\boldsymbol{x}\in\{0,1\}^n$ on a small instance and asserts $\boldsymbol{x}^\top Q\boldsymbol{x} + \mathrm{off} = C(\boldsymbol{x})$; another substitutes $\boldsymbol{z} = 1-2\boldsymbol{x}$ into the Ising form and checks it reproduces the QUBO energy; a third verifies that the $k$-th entry of the diagonal cost Hamiltonian agrees with the Ising energy of bitstring $k$. Together these catch the silent sign-flip bugs in the $\{0,1\}\!\leftrightarrow\!\{-1,+1\}$ change of variables.

\paragraph{Classical baselines.} One check asserts that the brute-force solution is binary and satisfies $\sum_i x_i = K$; a second runs greedy-Sharpe, Markowitz-rounded and simulated annealing and confirms $C_{\text{BF}} \leq C_{\text{solver}}$ up to $10^{-12}$, pinning down a single ground-truth cost oracle; a third fixes the dictionary of solver names exposed by the unified baseline runner.

\paragraph{QAOA energy and probabilities.} One check asserts that the minimum eigenvalue of $H_C$ equals the brute-force cost on a small instance, ruling out unit and sign mismatches between $H_C$ and $C(\boldsymbol{x})$. A second verifies $\langle +|^{\otimes n} H_C |+\rangle^{\otimes n} = \mathrm{mean}(H_C)$ at $(\gamma,\beta)=(0,0)$, confirming that the statevector kernel evaluates the right expectation. A third requires the scaled approximation ratio to satisfy $r\geq 0.95$ on $n=4$ at $p=10$ with fifteen restarts, which would fail if either the cost Hamiltonian or the mixer were mis-implemented.

\paragraph{Constraint-preserving mixer.} The XY-ring mixer is operationally useful only if it commutes with the Hamming-weight operator $\hat N = \sum_i \tfrac{1}{2}(I-\hat Z_i)$; otherwise the dynamics leaks out of the weight-$K$ subspace and the Dicke initial state stops being feasible. The test asserts $\|[H_M^{(XY)}, \hat N]|\psi\rangle\| \leq 10^{-12}$ on a Haar-random superposition, and that $\langle D^n_K|\hat N|D^n_K\rangle = K$ before and after a finite-$\beta$ XY mixing step. The Dicke preparation is verified to have unit norm and the expected support on the $\binom{n}{K}$ weight-$K$ basis states.

\paragraph{MIP exact-match check.} The PuLP-CBC mixed-integer programme of \cref{subsec:codestructure} solves the same cost function, with a hard cardinality constraint replacing the QUBO's $A$-penalty and the variance term $\boldsymbol x^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\boldsymbol x$ linearised by the standard McCormick--Glover construction \parencite{glover1975integerprog}. The test asserts that the CBC optimum agrees with brute force to floating-point precision on a small instance, anchoring the QAOA gap reported in \cref{tab:classical-baselines} against an industry-standard exact solver rather than only against brute force.

Together these checks pin down the cost function, the Hamiltonians, and the reported metric to the same numerical reference, leaving the QAOA results in \cref{section:result} to be read as statements about the optimiser, the ansatz, and the mixer choice rather than about the bookkeeping.
