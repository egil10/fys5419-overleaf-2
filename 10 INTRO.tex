% --- 1.1  Motivation -------------------------------------------------
% TODO: Markowitz (1952) mean--variance is one of the foundational
% problems in finance. With a cardinality constraint ``pick exactly $K$
% out of $n$ assets'', the continuous quadratic programme becomes a
% binary quadratic programme — formally a QUBO — which is NP-hard in
% general. Brute-force enumeration scales as $2^n$: tractable for the
% four-asset problems studied here ($2^4 = 16$ evaluations), infeasible
% for the typical institutional universe of hundreds of assets. The
% same QUBO form recurs across combinatorial optimisation (MAX-CUT,
% travelling salesman, graph colouring, lattice protein folding), so
% a general-purpose QUBO solver is by extension a general-purpose
% combinatorial solver, which is the broader reason the algorithmic
% question matters.
% \autocite{markowitz1952}

% --- 1.2  Why QAOA ---------------------------------------------------
% TODO: Variational quantum algorithms such as QAOA \autocite{farhi2014qaoa}
% reformulate combinatorial optimisation as a ground-state search of an
% Ising Hamiltonian. Three honest motivations make QAOA a natural object
% of study even before any quantum advantage has been demonstrated:
%   (a) Theoretical tractability. QAOA admits provable approximation
%       guarantees on structured problems. Farhi, Goldstone and
%       Gutmann (2014) proved an approximation ratio $r \geq 0.6924$
%       at $p = 1$ for MAX-CUT on 3-regular graphs — modest, but the
%       point is that the algorithm is mathematically tame enough for
%       such bounds to exist at all.
%   (b) Structural novelty. QAOA explores the full $2^n$-dimensional
%       Hilbert space in superposition; quantum interference lets
%       amplitude flow non-locally across the cost landscape in ways
%       that have no classical analogue. Whether this delivers an
%       actual asymptotic speedup is open.
%   (c) NISQ-compatibility. The QAOA circuit has $n$ qubits, depth
%       $O(p)$ and only $2p$ variational parameters, so unlike Shor's
%       algorithm or HHL it can in principle be executed on present-day
%       hardware without fault tolerance.
% Brief mention of recent work on quantum portfolio optimisation
% \autocite{stopfer2025benchmark,mancilla2026constrained,shen2025noisy,
%  morapakula2025annealing,thomassin2025slack,soloviev2025pauli}.
%
% Honest caveats — revisited in Section~\ref{section:conclusion}:
%   - No quantum advantage has yet been demonstrated for QAOA on a
%     useful problem; classical baselines (Goemans--Williamson for
%     MAX-CUT, Gurobi/CPLEX for portfolio selection) outperform QAOA
%     at the depths achievable on real devices today.
%   - The classical optimisation step is plagued by ``barren plateaus'':
%     the gradient of $E(\boldsymbol\gamma,\boldsymbol\beta)$ vanishes
%     exponentially in $n$ for random initialisations of sufficiently
%     deep circuits.
%   - The depth $p$ required to compete with classical heuristics on
%     hard instances appears to grow with system size, eroding the
%     NISQ-friendliness in practice.
% We therefore treat QAOA as a research vehicle that lets us pose
% sharp questions about the cost-landscape geometry, the dependence
% on depth, and the breakdown of adiabaticity — not as a tool we
% expect to beat Gurobi on a four-asset problem.
