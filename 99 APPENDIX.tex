\subsection*{A. Notation and conventions}\label{subsec:app_notation}

Symbols are used consistently throughout the report, in line with \textcite{iso80000_2}. Vectors are typeset bold lower case ($\boldsymbol{x}$, $\boldsymbol\mu$), matrices bold upper case ($\boldsymbol\Sigma$, $Q$), and components carry an index ($x_i$, $\mu_i$, $\Sigma_{ij}$). Operators acting on the qubit Hilbert space carry a hat ($\hat{Z}_i$, $\hat{X}_i$); the cost and mixer Hamiltonians $H_C, H_M$ are written without hats because they are sums of hatted single-qubit operators and the typographical distinction is unambiguous in context. \Cref{tab:notation} collects every recurring symbol with its definition and the equation or section in which it is introduced.

Unless a caption says otherwise, every numerical experiment in \cref{section:result} uses the \emph{canonical instance} fixed by \texttt{portfolio.DEFAULTS}: $n=16$, $K=4$, $\lambda=2.0$, $A=0.5$, $p=3$, ten random COBYLA multi-starts, master seed $42$. Qubit $0$ is taken to be the most significant bit of the computational-basis index, matching the order in which the QUBO matrix is built and the Ising spins are addressed in \texttt{ising.py}. Log axes are used in figures where the underlying quantity spans more than a single decade (runtime, $1-r$, $p_{\text{opt}}$); linear axes are used otherwise.

\subsection*{F. Background literature}

The reference list collected during this project covers several overlapping themes; every bibliography entry is cited at least once here so that the reference list can be audited and pruned. Each citation is given individually rather than bundled, so that an author-year reader can match line by line.

\paragraph{Foundational quantum mechanics.}
The original EPR paper \parencite{epr1935} sets the historical context for entanglement, which underpins the interference mechanism exploited by QAOA. Early demonstrations of small-scale quantum computation on molecular spin systems were reported by \textcite{gershenfeld_qc_molecules}.

\paragraph{Quantum computing textbooks.}
General textbook references include the foundational \textcite{nielsen_chuang}, the applied treatment of \textcite{hidary_qc_applied}, the programmer-oriented exposition of \textcite{hundt_qc_programmers}, the formal mathematics of \textcite{scherer_math_qc}, the introductory text of \textcite{olivares_student_qc}, the early survey of \textcite{rieffel1998introqc}, and the Qiskit-flavoured course of \textcite{loredo_learn_qc_python_ibm}. Quantum machine learning specifically is covered by \textcite{schuld_qml}, \textcite{conti_qml} and \textcite{pattanayak_qml_python}.

\paragraph{QAOA and the variational paradigm.}
The QAOA algorithm itself is due to \textcite{farhi2014qaoa}, with the depth and landscape analysis of \textcite{zhou2018qaoa}. Pedagogical introductions are given by \textcite{giovagnoli_qaoa_intro}, by \textcite{irfan_qaoa_simulators} for the hardware-vs-simulator comparison, and by the IBM Quantum tutorial \parencite{ibm_qaoa_tutorial}. The closely related variational quantum eigensolver framework is reviewed by \textcite{tilly2022vqe}.

\paragraph{Many-body solvable models.}
The Lipkin--Meshkov--Glick model \parencite{lipkin1965} provides a small, exactly solvable many-body Hamiltonian routinely used as a VQE benchmark in the FYS5419 course.

\paragraph{Markowitz and quantum portfolio optimisation.}
Modern portfolio theory originates with \textcite{markowitz1952}; statistical-learning context for return estimation is found in \textcite{hastie}. Recent quantum approaches to constrained portfolio selection are reported by \textcite{knapsack2024qaoa} for a knapsack formulation, by \textcite{thomassin2025slack} for slack variables, by \textcite{mancilla2026constrained} for XY-mixers, by \textcite{stopfer2025benchmark} for a systematic benchmark, by \textcite{shen2025noisy} for noisy-device simulations, by \textcite{morapakula2025annealing} for annealing comparisons, by \textcite{soloviev2025pauli} for a Pauli-correlation encoding, and by \textcite{soloviev2025cutting} for circuit-cutting techniques.

\paragraph{Notation and standards.}
Mathematical notation conventions follow the ISO 80000-2 standard \parencite{iso80000_2}.

\paragraph{Software libraries.}
The implementations consulted while developing the code base include Qiskit \parencite{qiskit}, PennyLane \parencite{pennylane}, QuTiP \parencite{qutip} with its source on GitHub \parencite{qutip_github}, PySCF \parencite{pyscf}, and the qBraid platform \parencite{qbraid}.

\paragraph{Course teaching material.}
The full set of FYS5419 weekly teaching material, hand-written notes, and lecture videos is hosted in the public course repository maintained by \textcite{qcm2026_coursepage}; supplementary workshop material is provided by \textcite{quantgates_workshop}.
