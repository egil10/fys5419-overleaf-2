\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We apply the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) to the cardinality-constrained mean--variance portfolio selection problem. Markowitz's objective with a fixed-budget constraint is reformulated as a QUBO, mapped to an Ising cost Hamiltonian, and minimised by a variational circuit alternating cost and mixer unitaries at depths $p = 1, 2, 3, 4$. We benchmark the algorithm on a synthetic four-asset dataset and on real S\&P~500 return data, validating every run against an exact brute-force solution. The energy landscape $E(\gamma, \beta)$ is mapped at $p = 1$, the approximation ratio is tracked as a function of circuit depth, and the optimiser's restart variance is quantified. As a methodological contribution we compare the conventional penalty-based encoding of the cardinality constraint to a Hamming-weight-preserving XY-mixer that enforces $\sum_i x_i = K$ by construction. Finally, we stress-test the algorithm on covariance matrices estimated during the 2008 financial crisis, the 2020 pandemic shock, and the 2022 rate-spike window, where the eigenvalue spectrum of $\Sigma$ flattens and the classical Markowitz solution becomes unstable. We discuss the relative merits of QAOA depth, mixer choice, and penalty calibration, and outline directions for scaling beyond the few-qubit regime.
\end{abstract}
