\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Picking $K$ assets from a universe of $n$ to balance risk and return is a textbook problem that turns NP-hard the moment cardinality is enforced, and it has become a flagship test case for near-term quantum optimisation. We apply the Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm \parencite{farhi2014qaoa} to the cardinality-constrained mean--variance portfolio selection problem of Markowitz, implemented from scratch as a pure-NumPy statevector simulator without Qiskit, PennyLane, or any external quantum framework. The objective is reformulated as a QUBO with budget penalty $A$, mapped to an Ising cost Hamiltonian built from estimated mean vector $\boldsymbol\mu$ and covariance $\boldsymbol\Sigma$, and minimised by a variational circuit alternating cost and transverse-field mixer unitaries, with COBYLA driving fifty random multi-starts. The canonical instance fixes $n=16$ equities drawn from the mag7, quantum, quantum-big, and anti baskets over 751 trading days from 2023-01-03 to 2025-12-30, at $K=4$, $\lambda=2.0$, $A=0.5$, and depth $p=3$. Seven experiments are reported: a canonical single-run benchmark; a $p=1$ energy-landscape map with all restart endpoints overlaid; a depth sweep $p\in\{1,\dots,5\}$; a warm-start ablation in the style of \textcite{zhou2018qaoa}; a risk-aversion sweep across three decades of $\lambda$; an eight-point budget-penalty sweep over $A\in[10^{-2}, 10^{1.5}]$; a size-scaling sweep $n\in\{4,\dots,16\}$; and a constraint-preserving XY-ring mixer benchmarked head-to-head against the transverse-field mixer at $n=8$, $K=2$. Classical baselines --- brute-force enumeration, greedy-Sharpe, rounded continuous Markowitz, simulated annealing, and an exact mixed-integer programme solved by CBC --- all converge in milliseconds to the same optimal basket $\{\text{GOOGL}, \text{IBM}, \text{NVDA}, \text{JPM}\}$ at $C(\boldsymbol{x}^{\star})=-1.40\times 10^{-3}$. The standard penalty QAOA at $p=3$ reaches a scaled approximation ratio $r\approx 0.99$ with fifty restarts but concentrates probability mass on infeasible bitstrings --- the all-ones state and single-asset quantum-stock states --- rather than the optimum, leaving $p_{\text{opt}}\approx 1.3\times 10^{-4}$ against a classical wall-clock advantage of roughly $4000\times$. Two design choices close most of the gap: warm-started angles restore monotone $r$-versus-$p$ scaling up to $r=0.997$ at $p=5$, and the XY-ring mixer paired with a Dicke initial state reaches $r=0.9995$ at every depth $p\in\{1,\dots,4\}$ with $p_{\text{feas}}=1$ by construction. No quantum advantage is claimed; the contribution is methodological --- a fully exposed pipeline that locates the exact failure mode of penalty QAOA on cardinality-constrained Markowitz and identifies two structural fixes.
\end{abstract}
