\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Picking $K$ assets from a universe of $n$ to balance risk and return is a textbook problem that turns NP-hard the moment cardinality is enforced, and it has become a flagship test case for near-term quantum optimisation. We apply the Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm \parencite{farhi2014qaoa} to the cardinality-constrained mean--variance portfolio selection problem of Markowitz, implemented from scratch as a pure-NumPy statevector simulator without Qiskit, PennyLane, or any external quantum framework. The objective is reformulated as a QUBO with budget penalty $A$, mapped to an Ising cost Hamiltonian built from estimated mean vector $\boldsymbol\mu$ and covariance $\boldsymbol\Sigma$, and minimised by a variational circuit alternating cost and transverse-field mixer unitaries, with COBYLA driving ten random multi-starts. The canonical instance fixes $n=16$ equities drawn from the mag7, quantum, quantum-big, and anti baskets over 751 trading days from 2023-01-03 to 2025-12-30, at $K=4$, $\lambda=2.0$, $A=0.5$, and depth $p=3$. Four sweeps probe depth, problem size, risk aversion, and penalty strength, complemented by a full $p=1$ energy-landscape map and brute-force, greedy-Sharpe, rounded-Markowitz, and simulated-annealing baselines, all three of which converge in milliseconds to the same optimal basket $\{\text{GOOGL}, \text{IBM}, \text{NVDA}, \text{JPM}\}$ at $C(x^{\star})=-1.40\times 10^{-3}$. QAOA reaches a scaled ratio $r\approx 0.94$, yet concentrates probability mass on infeasible bitstrings --- the all-ones state and single-asset quantum-stock states --- rather than the optimum, leaving $p_{\text{opt}}\approx 1.6\times 10^{-4}$ against a classical wall-clock advantage of roughly $4000\times$. No quantum advantage is claimed; the contribution is methodological.
\end{abstract}
