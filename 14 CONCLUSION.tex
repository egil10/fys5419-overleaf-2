\section{Conclusions and Perspectives}\label{section:conclusion}

\subsection{Main findings}

The eight experiments on the canonical $n=16$, $K=4$ instance and the classical baselines support six observations.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item All five classical baselines recover the same four-asset basket $\{\text{GOOGL}, \text{IBM}, \text{NVDA}, \text{JPM}\}$ (\cref{tab:classical-baselines}), with CBC certifying the optimum in well under a second. The cardinality-constrained portfolio is NP-hard in general, but at $n=16$ it is not a hard \emph{instance}.
    \item Standard penalty/X-mixer QAOA at $p=3$ scores $r \approx 0.99$, but this is a vanity ratio. The denominator $E_{\text{worst}} - E_{\text{opt}}$ is inflated by the all-ones state at penalty $AK^2$ (\cref{fig:cost-spectrum}), and the five most probable bitstrings are all infeasible (\cref{tab:top5}). The Hamming-weight mass (\cref{fig:hamming-mass}) tracks the uniform-mixer binomial baseline, confirming that the chosen penalty does not concentrate the state on the feasible subspace.
    \item Solution quality is flat across three orders of magnitude in $\lambda$, with a modest improvement at high $\lambda$ where the cost landscape flattens. Risk aversion is not the bottleneck on this instance.
    \item The depth sweep with random multi-start is non-monotonic, but the warm-start protocol of \cref{eq:warmstart} restores monotone $r$-versus-$p$ scaling (\cref{fig:warm-vs-random}). The non-monotonicity is a restart-budget artefact in the $(2\pi)^{2p}$ angle volume, not a property of the QAOA ansatz at this scale.
    \item The penalty sweep identifies a feasibility-mass peak near $A \approx 0.32$. Below that the penalty is dominated by the data scale; above it the penalty flattens the cost surface on the feasible subspace and feasibility decays. The canonical $A=0.5$ sits just to the right of the peak.
    \item The XY-ring mixer with a Dicke initial state achieves near-unit $r$ at every depth tested, with feasibility unity by construction (\cref{fig:xy-mixer}). The cost is an order-of-magnitude slower per-layer simulator runtime; on hardware that gap would close because the XY-mixer is implemented by gate decomposition rather than Krylov iteration.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Method-level discussion}

Methodologically, statevector simulation gives a clean, noise-free picture of QAOA dynamics, and brute force plus the CBC MIP supply unambiguous ground truth against which approximation ratios are reported honestly. The fifty-restart budget was decisive: several apparent dips in the risk and penalty sweeps that the earlier draft flagged as restart variance vanished at the larger budget, and the depth-sweep non-monotonicity was correctly identified as structural-with-a-fix rather than intrinsic to the ansatz.

The drawbacks are equally honest. At $n \leq 16$ the problem is classically trivial: brute force and CBC both finish in milliseconds, orders of magnitude faster than QAOA. The scaled ratio $r$ is misleading under penalty encoding: $r=0.99$ coexists with probability mass piling onto infeasible bitstrings (\cref{tab:top5}). The XY-mixer fix is clean and decisive but pays a simulator cost from the sparse matrix exponentiation; whether that cost survives on hardware depends on how the XY ring compiles into native two-qubit gates. QAOA at this scale is therefore valuable for the questions it lets us ask rather than for answers it provides over CBC on 16-asset portfolios.

\subsection{Future directions}

Four concrete next steps follow from the limitations above. First, \emph{stack warm starts on top of the XY-mixer}: both ablations close part of the gap independently and \cref{eq:warmstart} is mixer-agnostic, so a warm-started XY-Dicke pipeline would test whether the residual gap comes from the cost-layer angle space or from the mixer. Second, push the XY-mixer benchmark to the canonical $n=16$, $K=4$ via a Trotterised ring that decomposes into pairwise XX+YY rotations. Third, extend to inequality budgets $\sum_i x_i \leq K_{\max}$ via slack variables \parencite{thomassin2025slack}. Looking further out, circuit cutting \parencite{soloviev2025cutting} and Pauli-correlation encodings \parencite{soloviev2025pauli} would push past the $2^n$ statevector barrier, and a direct comparison with quantum annealing \parencite{morapakula2025annealing} would situate the results in a broader quantum landscape.
