\section{Conclusions and Perspectives}\label{section:conclusion}

\subsection{Main findings}

The seven parameter and ansatz sweeps, the $p=1$ landscape map with restart endpoints, and the classical baselines on the canonical $n=16$, $K=4$ instance support eight concrete observations.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Brute-force enumeration, greedy-Sharpe, rounded Markowitz, the simulated-annealing median, and the CBC mixed-integer programme all recover the same four-asset basket $\{\text{GOOGL}, \text{IBM}, \text{NVDA}, \text{JPM}\}$ at Sharpe $0.136$ and $C(\boldsymbol x^\star)=-1.40\times 10^{-3}$, in milliseconds and to numerical precision (\cref{tab:classical-baselines}). The cardinality-constrained portfolio is a textbook NP-hard problem, but at $n=16$ it is not a hard \emph{instance}.
    \item The CBC MIP returns the certified optimum in $175$~ms with a $0\%$ MIP gap, providing an industry-standard exact-solver anchor on top of the brute-force ground truth. The QAOA wall-clock at $p=3$ is roughly $750\times$ slower than CBC.
    \item Standard penalty/X-mixer QAOA at $p=3$ scores a scaled ratio $r \approx 0.99$ with fifty restarts that is largely a ``vanity ratio'': the denominator $E_{\text{worst}} - E_{\text{opt}}$ is inflated by the all-ones state at the budget penalty $AK^2$ (visualised in \cref{fig:cost-spectrum}), while the absolute energy sits at $\sim 500\times$ the optimum.
    \item The five most probable bitstrings at $p=3$ are all infeasible: rank 1 is the all-ones state at $0.7\%$, ranks 2--5 are the single quantum-stock states (\cref{tab:top5}). The brute-force optimum sits at rank $\sim 125$ with $p_{\text{opt}} = 1.6\times 10^{-4}$. The Hamming-weight distribution (\cref{fig:hamming-mass}) tracks the uniform-mixer binomial baseline at every weight, confirming that the budget penalty $A=0.5$ does not concentrate the variational state on the feasible subspace.
    \item Solution quality of standard QAOA is essentially flat across three orders of magnitude in the risk-aversion parameter $\lambda$, with a modest improvement at high $\lambda$ where the variance term dominates and the cost landscape flattens; risk aversion is not the bottleneck on this instance.
    \item The depth sweep with random multi-start is non-monotonic ($r$ peaks at $0.996$ for $p=2$, falls to $0.975$ at $p\in\{4,5\}$), but the warm-start protocol of \cref{eq:warmstart} restores monotone $r$-versus-$p$ scaling, climbing to $r=0.9968$ at $p=5$ (\cref{fig:warm-vs-random}). The non-monotonicity is therefore a restart-budget artefact in the $(2\pi)^{2p}$ angle volume, not a property of the QAOA ansatz at this scale.
    \item The penalty sweep across $A \in [10^{-2}, 10^{1.5}]$ identifies a feasibility-mass peak at $A \approx 0.32$ where $p_{\text{feas}}=0.91$; below that, the penalty is dominated by the data scale, above it the penalty term flattens the cost surface on the feasible subspace and feasibility decays back to $\sim 0.5$. The canonical $A=0.5$ sits just to the right of the peak.
    \item The constraint-preserving XY-ring mixer with a Dicke initial state achieves $r = 0.9995$ at every depth $p \in \{1,2,3,4\}$ at $n=8$, $K=2$, with $p_{\text{feas}} = 1$ by construction (\cref{fig:xy-mixer}). The cost is an order-of-magnitude slower per-layer simulator runtime from \texttt{expm\_multiply} on the sparse $2^n \times 2^n$ ring Hamiltonian; on hardware, the differential would close because the XY-mixer's matrix exponential is implemented by gate decomposition rather than by Krylov subspace iteration.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Method-level discussion}

The exercise has clear methodological merits. Statevector simulation produces a clean, noise-free picture of QAOA dynamics suited to pedagogy, and the modular pipeline $(\boldsymbol\mu, \boldsymbol\Sigma) \rightarrow Q \rightarrow (h, J, c) \rightarrow H_C$ is dataset-agnostic. The variational principle $E \geq E_0$ supplies a built-in correctness anchor, and brute-force enumeration at $n \leq 16$ supplemented by the CBC MIP gives unambiguous ground truth against which approximation ratios can be reported honestly rather than relative to a moving target \parencite{farhi2014qaoa}. Bumping the random-multi-start budget from ten to fifty was decisive: several apparent dips in the risk and penalty sweeps that the earlier draft of this report had to flag as restart variance disappeared at the larger budget, and the depth-sweep non-monotonicity that survived the bump was correctly identified as structural-with-a-fix (warm starts) rather than as an intrinsic property of the ansatz.

The drawbacks are equally honest. At $n \leq 16$ the problem is classically trivial: brute force terminates in roughly $20~\text{ms}$ at $n=16$, while greedy-Sharpe, Markowitz-round, and CBC reach the optimum at machine precision in under $200$~ms, between $750\times$ and $4000\times$ faster than QAOA. The scaled ratio $r$ is a vanity number under the penalty encoding: a value of $0.99$ on a spectrum dominated by the penalty $AK^2$ coexists with an absolute energy hundreds of times the optimum, and with the algorithm's probability mass piling onto infeasible bitstrings (\cref{tab:top5}). The penalty mixer is also sensitive to $A$ in a way that is difficult to tune without enumerating $C(\boldsymbol x)$ (precisely the work QAOA is meant to avoid), though our denser penalty sweep does locate a $p_{\text{feas}}$ peak near $A \approx 0.32$ that suggests a more principled default. The XY-mixer fix is conceptually clean and empirically decisive but pays an order-of-magnitude simulator cost from \texttt{expm\_multiply}; whether that cost survives on real hardware depends on how the XY ring is compiled into native two-qubit gates.

The honest framing is therefore that QAOA at this scale is valuable for the questions it lets us ask (what $E(\boldsymbol\gamma, \boldsymbol\beta)$ looks like, how $r$ scales with $p$, where adiabaticity breaks down, and what design choices fix it) rather than for answers it provides over CBC on 16-asset portfolios. No quantum advantage is claimed. The substantive contribution is that a fully exposed pure-NumPy pipeline can isolate the failure mode of standard penalty QAOA (mass on infeasible bitstrings) and verify the two structural fixes named in the recent QAOA literature against the same anchor instance.

\subsection{Future directions}

Several concrete next steps would directly address the limitations identified above, forming a natural sequence from immediate fixes to longer-term comparisons. The most informative single experiment we did not run is \emph{stacking warm starts on top of the XY-mixer}: both ablations close part of the gap independently and \cref{eq:warmstart} is mixer-agnostic, so a warm-started XY-Dicke pipeline would test whether the residual $5\times 10^{-4}$ in $1-r$ at $p=4$ comes from the cost-layer angle space or from the mixer. Pushing the XY-mixer benchmark to the canonical $n=16$, $K=4$ instance would require either a more efficient sparse linear-algebra backend (the $65{,}536 \times 65{,}536$ ring Hamiltonian has $\sim 2 \times 10^5$ non-zeros, still tractable but slow per \texttt{expm\_multiply} call) or a Trotterised XY-mixer implementation that decomposes the ring into pairwise XX+YY rotations and replaces the dense \texttt{expm} with a sequence of factorised two-qubit unitaries. Extending to inequality budgets $\sum_i x_i \leq K_{\max}$ via slack variables \parencite{thomassin2025slack} is a natural follow-on once the equality case behaves well; the slack construction integrates cleanly with our QUBO builder and the MIP baseline. Scaling past the $2^n$ statevector barrier looks feasible through circuit cutting \parencite{soloviev2025cutting} and Pauli-correlation encodings \parencite{soloviev2025pauli}, which would open up problem sizes where the classical baselines are no longer trivial; benchmarking against quantum annealing on identical instances \parencite{morapakula2025annealing} would situate the results in a broader quantum landscape. A direct comparison against Gurobi MIP on the same instances would, finally, quantify the QAOA-vs-classical gap against a state-of-the-art commercial baseline rather than the open-source CBC; our PuLP plumbing makes that a one-line swap.

A final methodological point worth investigating is whether the modal-bitstring story of \cref{tab:top5} is universal across constrained-QAOA pipelines or specific to the X-mixer/penalty combination. Replacing the X-mixer alone (keeping the penalty) with a Heisenberg-style mixer that biases towards intermediate Hamming weights, or replacing the penalty alone (keeping the X-mixer) with an inequality slack encoding, would partition the credit for the XY-Dicke improvement between the constraint preservation and the initial-state choice. The current experiment changes both simultaneously, so the report can only assert that the combination works.
